# How hedge funds and professional investors predict political, legislative, regulatory and geopolitical decisions

Scope: methods, data, industry players and academic evidence for forecasting non-central-bank policy outcomes (bills, regulators such as FDA/FCC/FTC/DOJ/SEC/CMS, tariffs and trade, sanctions, elections, court rulings, foreign policy). Current as of 15 October 2026; dates are given for older facts. Mostly US, with UK/EU notes where found.

Glossary (plain definitions, used throughout):
- **Basis point (bp):** 0.01 percentage point. 100 bp = 1%.
- **Abnormal return / alpha:** the return above what a benchmark or risk model predicts.
- **MNPI (material nonpublic information):** information a reasonable investor would consider important and that has not been made public. Trading on it in breach of a duty is insider trading.
- **Political intelligence (PI):** the STOCK Act's term for information gathered from direct contact with government officials and sold to investors.
- **Base rate / reference class:** how often something happened historically among similar cases (for example, "what share of introduced bills become law").
- **Implied probability:** the probability a market price implies. A prediction-market contract paying $1 if an event happens and trading at $0.70 implies about 70%.
- **Brier score:** a scoring rule for probability forecasts. It is the mean squared error between the forecast probability and the outcome (0 or 1). Lower is better.
- **Ideal point:** a legislator's or judge's estimated position on a left-right (or other) scale, estimated from their votes.
- **Event study:** measures abnormal stock returns in a short window around an announcement, to see how much news the market considered it.
- **Merger-arbitrage spread:** the gap between a target's share price and the deal price. A wider gap means the market sees more risk that the deal fails, often because of regulators.

## 1. What is the political intelligence industry, who are the players, and where are the legal and ethical lines?

### Takeaway
Funds buy Washington insight from three main sources. The first is independent "policy research" boutiques (Capital Alpha, Beacon Policy Advisors, Height Capital Markets, Signum). The second is sell-side policy teams (Strategas/Baird, Piper Sandler) and global political-risk consultancies (Eurasia Group). The third is lobbyists, former officials and expert networks. US law does not regulate PI directly. Instead, PI sits on top of general insider-trading law and the 2012 STOCK Act. Prosecutors' main criminal theory for trading on leaked agency information (Title 18 wire and securities fraud) was sharply narrowed by *United States v. Blaszczak* (2d Cir., Dec. 2022). Since 2025–26 the policy fight has moved to members' stock trading and to insider betting on prediction markets.

### Cited Findings

**Definition, size and regulation**
- The STOCK Act of 2012 defines political intelligence as information "derived by a person from direct communications with an executive branch employee, a Member of Congress, or an employee of Congress" and provided for compensation to a client who intends to use it for investment decisions. GAO's mandated study (GAO-13-389, April 4, 2013, titled *"Political Intelligence: Financial Market Value of Government Information Hinges on Materiality and Timing"*) found that **no law or ethics rule specifically governs PI** beyond that definition. Securities laws and branch ethics rules protect MNPI, and insider-trading prohibitions apply to both the executive and legislative branches. — [GAO-13-389 product page](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-13-389); [full PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-13-389.pdf)
- GAO said the size of the PI market is "unknown and hard to quantify." Firms bundle PI with industry research and policy analysis, and investors use many sources, so the effect of any single piece of information is "extremely difficult to measure." GAO also flagged that materiality and timing are often unclear: it is "not always possible to determine the timing of when nonpublic information becomes public." Most PI firms GAO interviewed said they have policies against knowingly selling MNPI. GAO made no recommendations and noted a "lack of consensus on the meaning of the terms" *direct communication* and *investment decision*. — [GAO-13-389](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-13-389)
- One industry-size estimate: political intelligence brought in **$402 million in 2009**, per Integrity Research Associates (cited by Mother Jones, Nov. 2013). PI firms are not required to disclose their agents or clients. — [Mother Jones, "The Fastest-Growing Washington Industry You've Never Heard Of" (2013)](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2013/11/political-intelligence-industry-jellyfish/)
- Legislative history: Rep. Louise Slaughter first introduced the STOCK Act in 2006. That version would have required PI firms to register and disclose clients and payments, much like lobbyists. The final 2012 law dropped the registration provision after heavy lobbying and instead ordered the GAO study. — [Mother Jones (2013)](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2013/11/political-intelligence-industry-jellyfish/); [GAO-13-389 summary search result / Caplin & Drysdale summary](https://www.caplindrysdale.com/media/publication/150503_8455_GAO_Report_Highlights_Concerns_Over_Political_Intelligence_Disclosure.pdf)
- Under the STOCK Act, government officials owe a duty of trust and confidence, which exposes hedge funds that trade on private political information to potential insider-trading liability. — [Hedge Fund Law Report, "Political Intelligence Firms and the STOCK Act"](https://www.hflawreport.com/2543531/political-intelligence-firms-and-the-stock-act-how-hedge-fund-managers-can-avoid-potential-pitfalls.thtml)
- Historical examples: in 1984 Ivan Boesky reportedly hired lobbyists to sit in on committee hearings about a big oil merger. In 2005 hedge funds bought stocks of companies that a proposed $140bn asbestos trust fund would shield, shortly before Sen. Bill Frist announced a vote, and the SEC suspected a leak from his office. — [Mother Jones (2013)](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2013/11/political-intelligence-industry-jellyfish/)

**Case 2: Blaszczak (CMS leaks to a PI consultant and Deerfield)**
- David Blaszczak, a former CMS employee turned PI consultant, allegedly obtained confidential details of upcoming Medicare reimbursement decisions (May 2012–Nov 2013) from CMS employee Christopher Worrall. He passed them to clients, including two Deerfield Management partners. All four were convicted by a jury in May 2018. Deerfield analyst Jordan Fogel cooperated and settled with the SEC. — [SECActions (charges)](https://secactions.com/sec-usao-charge-four-with-insider-trading-tied-to-political-intelligence); [SECActions (verdict)](https://www.secactions.com/political-intelligence-consultant-cms-official-guilty-of-insider-trading/); [SEC Lit. CRS notes "there is no federal statute that specifically targets insider trading," so this narrowing matters. — [CRS Legal Sidebar LSB10955 (Apr. 2023)](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/LSB10955.html); [Morrison Foerster](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/230123-second-circuit-nixes-theory); [Simpson Thacher](https://www.stblaw.com/about-us/publications/view/2023/01/03/second-circuit-curtails-title-18-insider-trading-liability)

### Inferences
- The legal line in practice: funds may buy *analysis* (synthesis of public statements, procedural mechanics, vote counts, read-throughs from former officials about how an agency typically decides), but not *leaked decisions*. GAO's point that materiality and timing are blurry is the core compliance problem. A note saying "CMS will reverse the cut," sent 30 minutes before the announcement, looks very different from a note saying "the politics favor a reversal."
- After *Blaszczak II*, prosecuting a tip of confidential agency information relies mainly on classic Exchange Act §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 theories. Those require a breach of duty and, for tippees, a personal benefit to the tipper. This is my reading of the commentary and should be checked against the CRS sidebar's full text. The SEC's civil powers remain, and the 2015 Gardephe ruling confirms congressional staff are not immune.
- The industry's "edge" claims are mostly self-reported: Institutional Investor/Extel rankings and firm bios. I found no independent, audited track record for any named PI or policy-research firm.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 market-size estimate for PI found. The $402m figure is from 2009.
- I did not confirm the final outcome of the Height Securities / Ways & Means probe after the 2015 ruling. I found no public charges.
- The Greenberg Traurig lobbyist's name (widely reported as Mark Hayes) was not confirmed in a retrieved article.
- Not covered: UK/EU equivalents of PI (for example, Brussels consultancies) and the rules on them. China-focused policy research shops were not researched.
- Whether the Senate has since acted on the Young–Slotkin or Torres prediction-market bills was not checked past April–May 2026.

## 2. What does academic research show about whether policy information is valuable and traded on?

### Takeaway
Strong evidence shows that (a) political uncertainty is priced, with option premia around elections and summits and risk premia larger in bad times, (b) firms' political connections and contributions predict returns, and (c) hedge funds connected to lobbyists earned abnormal returns on politically sensitive stocks that shrank after the STOCK Act. Evidence that *members of Congress themselves* beat the market is mixed. Older studies (1990s data) found large excess returns, but newer studies using 2004–2020 data find none on average.

### Cited Findings

**Hedge funds and lobbyists**
- **Gao, Meng & Jiekun Huang (2016), "Capitalizing on Capitol Hill: Informed trading by hedge fund managers," *Journal of Financial Economics* 121(3): 521–545, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2015.11.001.** Using hedge funds' long-equity holdings and their links to lobbyists (1999–2012), they find that connected funds beat passive benchmarks by **56–93 bp per month** on their "political holdings," and that this outperformance **fell significantly after the STOCK Act took effect** (2012). The proposed mechanism: lobbyists swap information with legislators, and many lobbyists are former legislators or staff. — [Harvard Law School Forum summary (2016)](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2016/08/26/capitalizing-on-capitol-hill-informed-trading-by-hedge-fund-managers/); [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/capitalizing-on-capitol-hill-informed-trading-by-hedge-fund-manag/)
  - Version note: a 2010 working-paper version (1999–2008 data) was reported as showing 1.6–2.5% per month outperformance. Cite the published JFE figures. — [Cato, "The Ultimate Insiders" (2010)](https://www.cato.org/commentary/ultimate-insiders)
- **Ainsworth, Scott et al. (2016), "Insuring hedged bets with lobbying":** finds that hedge-fund lobbying spending tracks hedge-fund assets under management and federal budget growth. A related Harvard Dataverse dataset covers 1998–2018. I did not verify the venue. — [Scott Ainsworth papers (Academia.edu)](https://uga.academia.edu/ScottAinsworth)
- After the financial crisis, hedge-fund lobbying shifted from *preventing* regulation (such as SEC registration) to *shaping* it (Dodd-Frank). — [Hedge Fund Law Report](https://hflawreport.com/article/442)

**Political connections and contributions**
- **Cooper, Michael J., Huseyin Gulen & Alexei V. Ovtchinnikov (2010), "Corporate Political Contributions and Stock Returns," *Journal of Finance* 65(2): 687–724, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01548.x.** Using a firm-level database of PAC contributions (1979–2004), they find contribution measures are positively and significantly related to future stock returns, and the effect is strongest for firms supporting more home-state office-holders. One summary puts it as roughly +0.5% monthly return per one-standard-deviation increase in the number of candidates supported. — [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v65y2010i2p687-724.html); [Harvard Law School Forum (2009)](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2009/08/17/corporate-political-contributions-and-stock-returns/)
- **Faccio, Mara (2006), "Politically Connected Firms," *American Economic Review* 96(1): 369–386, DOI 10.1257/000282806776157704.** A foundational cross-country paper showing that political connections are valuable. I retrieved only the bibliographic record, not the specific results. — [cited in literature review (EFMA 2011 paper)](https://efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2011-Braga/papers/0236.pdf)

**Political uncertainty is priced**
- **Pástor, Ľuboš & Pietro Veronesi (2013), "Political uncertainty and risk premia," *JFE* 110(3): 520–545, DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.08.007.** In a general-equilibrium model, political uncertainty "commands a risk premium whose magnitude is larger in weaker economic conditions." It also reduces the value of the government's implicit "put" (downside protection), and makes stocks more volatile and more correlated, especially in downturns. The authors report empirical support. — [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v110y2013i3p520-545.html); [NBER w17464](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w17464/w17464.pdf)
- **Kelly, Bryan, Ľuboš Pástor & Pietro Veronesi (2016), "The Price of Political Uncertainty: Theory and Evidence from the Option Market," *Journal of Finance* 71(5): 2417–2480, DOI 10.1111/jofi.12406.** Equity options whose lives span national elections or global summits are more expensive. They hedge price, variance and tail risk tied to political events, and that protection is worth more in weak economies and when political uncertainty is high. The effects spill across countries. Data: options from about 20 countries since 1990. — [NBER w19812](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19812.pdf); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/measuring-uncertainty-helps-investors-navigate-risk)
- **Baker, Scott R., Nicholas Bloom & Steven J. Davis (2016), "Measuring Economic Policy Uncertainty," *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 131(4).** The US EPU index counts articles in 10 major newspapers that contain terms for "economy," "uncertainty," and one or more policy terms (Congress, deficit, Federal Reserve, legislation, regulation, White House). It was validated by human audits of 12,000 articles. Higher EPU is associated with higher stock volatility and lower investment and hiring in policy-sensitive sectors. Free data at policyuncertainty.com. — [QJE paper PDF](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~mchinn/bakerbloomdavis_QJE2016.pdf)
- Davis (2019, NBER w26243) highlights trade policy as a major source of uncertainty and stock-market volatility since March 2018 (US–China tensions). — [NBER w26243](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26243.pdf)

**Do members of Congress trade on what they know?**
- **Ziobrowski, Alan J., Ping Cheng, James W. Boyd & Brigitte J. Ziobrowski (2004), "Abnormal Returns from the Common Stock Investments of the U.S. Senate," *Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis* 39(4): 661–676.** For 1993–1998, a portfolio mimicking Senators' purchases beat the market by **85 bp/month**, and one mimicking their sales lagged by 12 bp/month. (The popular "senators beat the market by 12% a year" is a misreading.) — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/abnormal-returns-from-the-common-stock-investments-of-the-us-senate/A39406479940758D59E09FDCB8EE9BEC); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v39y2004i04p661-676_00.html)
- **Ziobrowski et al. (2011), "Abnormal returns from the common stock investments of members of the U.S. House of Representatives," *Business and Politics* 13(1):** House members' purchases beat the market by about 55 bp/month (about 6%/year). — [Lindenwood Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.lindenwood.edu/faculty-research-papers/240); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/bpjbuspol/v_3a13_3ay_3a2011_3ai_3a1_3an_3a4.htm)
- **Eggers, Andrew C. & Jens Hainmueller (2013), "Capitol Losses: The Mediocre Performance of Congressional Stock Portfolios," *Journal of Politics* 75(2).** For 2004–2008 they find no evidence of informed trading or above-market returns. The average member *underperformed* by 2–3%/year. — [MIT PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/Eggmueller_CapitolLosses.pdf); [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/46513)
- **Belmont, William, Bruce Sacerdote, Ranjan Sehgal & Ian Van Hoek (2022), "Do Senators and House Members Beat the Stock Market? Evidence from the STOCK Act," *Journal of Public Economics* 207: 104602.** Using STOCK Act trade disclosures (Jan 2012–Dec 2020), they find no superior returns overall or for senators accused of informed trading. House purchases underperformed by about 26 bp over six months. They caution that they cannot rule out individual insider trades hidden in averages. — [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pubeco/v207y2022ics0047272722000044.html); [NBER w26975](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26975.pdf)

**Regulatory event studies (examples of policy news moving prices)**
- The April 2013 Medicare Advantage rate reversal was flagged by Height's note as positive for insurers such as Humana and Health Net. The size of the price move was not retrieved here. — [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/judge-rules-for-sec-in-fight-with-house-panel-over-insider-trading-probe-paul-g/)
- After "Liberation Day" tariffs (Apr. 2, 2025), the S&P 500 fell almost 14% in less than a week. - Brexit: GBP traded above $1.50 on the eve of the June 23, 2016 vote and fell to about $1.32 as the Leave result became clear. — [News Letter](https://www.newsletter.co.uk/business/pollsters-under-fire-over-hedge-fund-brexit-bets-572972)

### Inferences
- The pattern across studies is that *channels* matter more than *status*. Connected hedge funds (Gao & Huang) and politically active firms (Cooper et al.) show measurable effects. Members of Congress, on average and after 2004, do not. A plausible reading is that the value lies in timely, specific regulatory information plus the skill to trade it, which professional funds have and most legislators lack.
- The fall in connected funds' alpha after the STOCK Act fits a story in which some of that edge came from information legislators or staff could no longer legally pass on. The authors present this as consistent with information flow, not proof of illegality.
- The Kelly–Pástor–Veronesi result implies a tradable regularity: options spanning scheduled political events carry a premium. Funds therefore choose between paying for event protection and selling it.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: Faccio (2006) specific estimates. Also not retrieved: firm-level studies of hedge funds trading around specific agency decisions (FCC spectrum, FDA, CMS) apart from those above, and research on lobbying disclosure data as a trading signal beyond Ainsworth et al.
- The venue for Ainsworth et al. (2016) was not confirmed.
- Not found: post-2020 academic evidence on PI-connected funds (for example, whether the post-STOCK Act decline persisted).

## 3. What quantitative methods do forecasters use (base rates, legislative models, ideal points, court and FDA prediction, election models)?

### Takeaway
The workhorse is (1) a base rate from a reference class, adjusted with (2) structural knowledge of veto points and actors' positions (ideal points, vote counts, agency track records) and (3) case-specific signals (bill text, cosponsors, advisory votes, oral argument). Machine-learning models of bill enactment and Supreme Court outcomes beat naive baselines modestly. Supreme Court models reach about 70–75% accuracy versus 59–66% for experts. Rare-event base rates dominate: only about 4–6% of introduced bills become law, and the FDA follows its advisory committees about 88% of the time.

### Cited Findings

**Base rates and reference classes**
- Only about 4–6% of bills introduced in Congress become law. Skopos Labs' model gave the 2017 ACA-replacement bill 15%, described as high relative to that base rate. — [Vanderbilt Engineering (2017)](https://engineering.vanderbilt.edu/news/2017/john-nay-ai-predictgov/); [Vanderbilt News](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2017/04/07/curious-congress-predictgov/)

- Good Judgment Project training explicitly taught reference-class (base-rate) thinking and averaging, and it improved accuracy. — [Mellers et al. (2014) via APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/predicting-uncertain-events-on-a-global-scale.html)
- Antitrust base rates used by merger arbitrageurs: one analyst cites White & Case data that the FTC/DOJ rejected only about 1–2% of deals outright without remedy (2016–2019), versus a McKinsey estimate that 10–20% of large deals get blocked. The analyst then asks whether market-implied break odds are above or below those rates. — [Old Rope Research (Substack)](https://oldroperesearch.substack.com/p/evaluating-subjective-probabilities)

**Legislative-outcome prediction**
- **Yano, Tae, Noah A. Smith & John D. - **Nay, John J. **Ideal points and vote models**

**Supreme Court prediction**

### Inferences
- A practical hedge-fund workflow, synthesized from the above: (1) start with the reference-class base rate (bill enactment, FDA–adcom concordance, antitrust challenge rates, Supreme Court reversal rate); (2) update with structural position data (NOMINATE ideal points and whip counts, commissioners' past votes, Justices' past votes); (3) update with case-specific signals (text, cosponsors, adcom vote, oral-argument tone, leaks or PI); (4) compare the result with the market-implied probability (option skew, merger spread, prediction-market price); (5) trade the gap.
- The CMS drug-selection miss shows a common failure: models built on stale public data when the decision-maker uses newer non-public data. The fix was to re-estimate from the statutory formula once later data were released, and accuracy then improved.
- Supreme Court and legislation models show that simple statistical models beat unaided experts mainly on "swing" actors and on rare-event calibration. Experts add value on institution-specific nuance.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed accuracy study found for commercial bill-forecasting tools (Skopos/PredictGov, FiscalNote). Accuracy claims are press-reported only.
- Not researched in depth: FCC/SEC rule-outcome prediction models, FDA approval-probability models (for example, Lo, Siah & Wong's machine-learning drug-approval work), and 2020/2024 election-model calibration.
- Final outcomes of the Capri/Tapestry and Albertsons/Kroger deals: my recollection is that both were blocked or abandoned in late 2024, which would make the low implied odds look prescient. I did not verify this here.

## 4. Prediction markets and crowd forecasting: what are they, how accurate are they, and how do funds use them (2024–2026)?

### Takeaway
Research since the 2000s finds that market-based and well-aggregated crowd forecasts are generally well calibrated and beat most benchmarks. Trained, teamed and tracked forecasters ("superforecasters") beat intelligence analysts in the IARPA tournament. Since Kalshi's September 2024 court win, US-regulated election and policy contracts have grown into a multibillion-dollar, increasingly institutional market: the CFTC dropped its appeal in May 2025, Polymarket re-entered the US via its QCEX purchase, and ICE invested. The 2026 Maduro and Iran episodes then made insider trading on these venues a live legislative issue.

### Cited Findings

**Academic evidence**
- **Wolfers, Justin & Eric Zitzewitz (2004), "Prediction Markets," *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 18(2): 107–126.** Across many settings, market forecasts "are typically fairly accurate, and … outperform most moderately sophisticated benchmarks." Well-designed contracts can reveal expected probabilities, means, medians and uncertainty. — [NCSU-hosted PDF](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/local/Social/f15/wrap/readings/Wolfers+Zitzewitz-Prediction-Markets.pdf); [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov04/can-markets-predict-future)
- **Arrow, Kenneth J. et al. (2008), "The Promise of Prediction Markets," *Science* 320(5878): 877–878.** Twenty-two economists, including Arrow, Milgrom, Shiller, Vernon Smith, Hanson, Wolfers and Zitzewitz, argued that prediction markets are a potent tool that "should be freed of unnecessary government restrictions." They cite improvements over polls, corporate experts and weather forecasts. — [Caltech record](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/vrtd6-e5w04); [Harvard Law bibliography](https://hls.harvard.edu/bibliography/economics-the-promise-of-prediction-markets)
- **Tetlock, Philip E. (2005), *Expert Political Judgment: How Good Is It? How Can We Know?* (Princeton UP).** Tetlock tracked about 284 experts over roughly 20 years. Most did no better than simple statistical algorithms, and many did worse. "Foxes" (eclectic thinkers) beat "hedgehogs" (one-big-idea thinkers), and fame was negatively related to accuracy. The "dart-throwing chimpanzee" line comes from this work. — [Jason Collins review](https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/expert-political-judgment); [Scholars' Stage](https://scholars-stage.org/?p=1275)
- **Mellers, Barbara et al. (2014), "Psychological Strategies for Winning a Geopolitical Forecasting Tournament," *Psychological Science* 25(5): 1106–1115.** In IARPA's tournament, where five university teams competed (the ACE program, 2011–2015), the Good Judgment Project won using **training** (probability reasoning, reference classes), **teaming** and **tracking** (putting top performers into elite "superforecaster" teams, who were far more accurate in year 2). A follow-up (Mellers, Stone, Atanasov et al. 2015, "The Psychology of Intelligence Analysis: Drivers of Prediction Accuracy in World Politics," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Applied*; I did not re-verify the venue in this pass) of 743 forecasters and more than 150,000 forecasts found cognitive ability, political knowledge and open-mindedness predicted accuracy. — [APS journal page](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797614524255/); [APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/predicting-uncertain-events-on-a-global-scale.html); [Wharton PDF (2015)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2015---the-psychology-of-intelligence-analysis.pdf)
  - Contested: a 2024 *Psychological Science* reanalysis ("Rethinking the Role of Teams and Training in Geopolitical Forecasting") argues the training and teaming effects shrink once extraneous variables are controlled. — [APS journal page (2024)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/09567976241266481/)
- Reported claim: GJP's top forecasters were about **30% more accurate** than intelligence-community analysts with access to classified data. This originated in David Ignatius's *Washington Post* reporting and is cited secondarily. Another version says GJP beat the IC's internal prediction market by 25–30%. Treat it as approximate. — [Wikipedia: Superforecaster](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Superforecaster); [EA Forum summary](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/W94KjunX3hXAtZvXJ/evidence-on-good-forecasting-practices-from-the-good)

### Inferences
- How funds use these venues: (a) as a free, real-time probability feed to compare against their own models and against option or credit pricing (implied-probability arbitrage); (b) increasingly as a direct hedging instrument for binary political risks (an election, a shutdown, a tariff deadline), now that CFTC-regulated venues exist and offer institutional and margin access; (c) as a market-making opportunity. I found no robust, primary documentation of *specific* named hedge funds hedging political risk on Kalshi or Polymarket. Most claims come from crypto-trade blogs.
- The 2026 insider episodes show the venues' signal can contain leaked MNPI. A sudden move from a new account is itself a "tell," and it carries legal risk for anyone acting on information they know came from an official.

### Gaps
- No independent 2024 calibration study (prediction markets vs. polls vs. models) was retrieved.
- Unconfirmed: named market makers (for example, Susquehanna) and the share of institutional volume. Claims like "40% institutional" appear only in low-quality blogs.
- Metaculus and PredictIt specifics (PredictIt's 2022–2025 CFTC litigation and status) were not researched in this pass.
- Exact Polymarket US relaunch timeline: sources conflict.

## 5. Which alternative data and text sources are used (regulatory trackers, comment analysis, NLP, lobbying disclosures, shipping and satellite, official statements)?

### Takeaway
Funds combine commercial legislative and regulatory trackers (FiscalNote/PolicyNote, Quorum, Bloomberg Government, Westlaw Edge with Skopos scores, GovTrack) with text-as-data. Examples are newspaper-based uncertainty indices, bill-text models and official social-media "tells." For geopolitics and sanctions they use maritime and satellite data (AIS ship tracking, SAR and optical imagery) to see whether sanctions actually bind. Private polling and exit polls are a special, controversial class (Brexit 2016).

### Cited Findings
- **Legislative and regulatory trackers:** FiscalNote's PolicyNote added AI bill forecasts and similar-bill detection in June 2025 (about 4,000 customers). Quorum provides AI bill tracking, summaries and semantic search. Westlaw Edge provides Skopos-based "Probability of Enactment" scores using more than 250 factors. GovTrack shows Skopos "prognosis" lines. — [FiscalNote release (Jun. 2025)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250612678578/en/FiscalNote-Accelerates-AI-Innovation-with-New-Enhancements-to-PolicyNote-for-Legislative-Forecasting-Alerts-and-Bill-Discovery); [FiscalNote vs Quorum](https://fiscalnote.com/blog/fiscalnote-vs-quorum); [ABA Journal](https://abajournal.com/news/article/new-feature-in-westlaw-edge-predicts-legislative-outcomes); [Vanderbilt Engineering](https://engineering.vanderbilt.edu/news/2017/john-nay-ai-predictgov/)
- **Regulations.gov comments:** agencies must consider and respond to "relevant and significant" comments when issuing a final rule, or risk losing in court. But "the public comment process is not a vote," and comment counts do not determine outcomes. Substantive, data-rich comments (often from regulated firms) are what shape final rules. — [Regulations.gov guide (PDF)](https://www.regulations.gov/assets/files/Public-Comment-on-Federal-Regulations_Final.pdf); [Data Foundation guide](https://datafoundation.org/news/evidence-capacity/875/875-EXPERT-ENGAGEMENT-GUIDE-Writing-Effective-Public-Comments-on-Federal-Rulemakings)
- **News-text indices:** the Baker–Bloom–Davis EPU index (with trade-policy, tax and other category sub-indices) is built from newspaper term counts and validated against 12,000 human-coded articles. — [Baker, Bloom & Davis QJE 2016](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~mchinn/bakerbloomdavis_QJE2016.pdf); [Davis NBER w26243](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26243.pdf)
- **Bill text as signal:** Yano–Smith–Wilkerson (2012) and Nay (2017) show that bill text adds predictive power beyond sponsor and committee. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/N12-1097/); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5425031/)
- **Officials' statements as "tells":** Trump's April 9, 2025 "GREAT TIME TO BUY" post came about four hours before the tariff pause. — [AP via WSLS](https://www.wsls.com/news/politics/2025/04/09/trumps-buy-tip-on-social-media-before-his-tariffs-pause-made-money-for-investors-who-listened/)
- **Market-stress feedback as a policy signal:** accounts of the April 2025 reversal cite pressure from business leaders and bond-market turmoil. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from about 4.0% to about 4.5% within days. — [The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2025/04/10/trump-tariff-pause-market-impact/); [Alaska Beacon / States Newsroom](https://alaskabeacon.com/2025/04/09/repub/trump-sets-90-day-pause-on-many-tariffs-hikes-china-to-125/)
- **Sanctions and "dark fleet" monitoring:** Windward (AI maritime analytics) flagged a surge in Russian tankers "going dark" (switching off AIS) after the 2022 invasion, for example at least 33 occurrences in one week. Maxar described a radar-then-optical method to find ships that switch off AIS. Planet's near-daily imagery helps track vessels without AIS. AIS spoofing (false positions) is common and can be detected from geometric track patterns. — [Claims Journal/Bloomberg (Mar. 2022)](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/international/2022/03/29/309507.htm); [Defense One (Sept. 2022)](https://defenseone.com/technology/2022/09/russian-oil-exports-rise-governments-and-shipping-companies-play-cat-and-mouse/376720); [Planet Pulse](https://www.planet.com/pulse/planets-data-leveraged-to-identify-dark-vessels-and-monitor-the-illicit-russian-oil-trade)
- A 2026 analysis estimates the shadow fleet at 2,500–3,000 vessels (June 2026), carrying about 7% of global crude flows in 2025. The search attributed this to an analysis linked from AAF or IBA, and I did not confirm the exact source. — [American Action Forum](https://www.americanactionforum.org/insight/oil-in-the-shadows-dark-shipping-evades-sanctions/); [IBA](https://ibanet.org/Russia-shadow-fleet-a-growing-threat)
- **Private polling / exit polls (UK, 2016):** hedge funds bought private polling from at least six firms (including YouGov, Survation and ICM) around the Brexit vote, including private on-the-day exit polls. These were legal under Electoral Commission rules as long as results were not published before polls closed at 10pm. Treasury Committee chair Nicky Morgan raised market-integrity concerns, and Lord Foulkes called it a "scandal." — [Bloomberg via BQ](https://www.bloombergquint.com/politics/u-k-lawmaker-says-secret-hedge-fund-brexit-polling-a-scandal); [News Letter](https://www.newsletter.co.uk/business/pollsters-under-fire-over-hedge-fund-brexit-bets-572972); [Irish News](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uknews/2016/06/01/news/financial-speculators-trying-to-profit-from-result-of-eu-referendum-541842/)
- **Lobbying and influence disclosures:** after Dodd-Frank, financial-industry filings citing Dodd-Frank as a lobbied issue totaled more than $330m (WSJ analysis). Americans for Financial Reform tracked private-equity and hedge-fund political spending in the 2020 cycle. — [ABI summary of WSJ](https://dev.abi.org/node/212463); [CNBC/NBC on AFR study](https://www.nbcsandiego.com/news/business/money-report/private-equity-hedge-funds-accounted-for-over-625-million-in-political-spending-during-2020-campaign-study-says-2/2762506/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- In practice, "alt data" for policy works on three timescales: (1) slow structural data (lobbying, contributions, ideal points, comment dockets) to set priors; (2) medium-frequency trackers and NLP (bill text changes, markup schedules, agency calendars, EPU-style indices) to update; (3) fast tells (official posts, sudden prediction-market moves, private polls, AIS anomalies) for timing.
- For sanctions, the question is often not "will sanctions be imposed" but "will they bind." Tanker-tracking data answers the second question, which is what moves oil spreads and freight rates.

## 6. What structured analytic techniques are used (scenarios, decision trees, actor/game-theory models, red-teaming), and how good are they?

### Takeaway
Professionals turn political questions into explicit probability trees and scenarios, and use stakeholder or actor models to estimate outcomes. The best-known formal actor model is Bruce Bueno de Mesquita's expected-utility ("Policon"/"predictioneer") model. A former CIA analyst reported it was about 90% accurate in internal comparisons, but that claim is self-reported, not independently audited, and lacks calibration data. The strongest independent evidence for structured technique comes from the IARPA forecasting tournaments: base rates, decomposition, teaming, frequent updating, and aggregation of many forecasters.

### Cited Findings
- **Bueno de Mesquita's model** asks experts for each stakeholder's preferred outcome, influence (clout) and salience (how much they care). It then simulates bargaining to predict the outcome. Former senior CIA analyst Stanley Feder said that in tests using forecasts made before events, "We found the model to be accurate 90 percent of the time," and that it was more specific than the agency's own analyses. — [Foreign Policy, "BDM, in profile" (2007)](https://foreignpolicy.com/2007/10/14/bdm-in-profile/); [Wikipedia (citing declassified CIA assessment)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Bueno_de_Mesquita)
- Feder's account: Stanley Feder, "Factions and Policon: New Ways to Analyze Politics," in H. Bradford Westerfield (ed.), *Inside CIA's Private World: Declassified Articles from the Agency's Internal Journal, 1955–1992* (Yale UP, 1995), originally in *Studies in Intelligence*. Commenters note the 90% figure is not independently verifiable, and that standard CIA methods were reportedly also about 90% accurate but less precise. — [LessWrong/GreaterWrong thread with citation](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/hMv7JhPMN8SuSrk7m/open-thread-april-2010/comment/Nsu9BYhePpMKiJ4k4); [CIA Reading Room: Studies in Intelligence collection](https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/collection/declassified-articles-studies-intelligence-ic%E2%80%99s-journal-intelligence-professional?page=7); [Technology Review profile of Feder](https://www.technologyreview.com/2006/08/08/228464/stanley-feder-66/amp/)
- A separate claim of a 97% hit rate on 21 European Community policy decisions is circulated for the model, but the evaluation was not described in the retrieved source. — [LessWrong "The New Nostradamus"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/XHjAxJvdpiqck8Da2/the-new-nostradamus)
- **Tournament-validated techniques:** probability training (reference classes, averaging), team debate, tracking elite forecasters, and aggregation improved Brier scores in IARPA's tournament. Tetlock's earlier work found eclectic "foxes" beat "hedgehogs." — [Mellers et al. 2014 (APS)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/0956797614524255/); [Jason Collins on Tetlock (2005)](https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/expert-political-judgment)
- **Scenario lists:** Eurasia Group's annual "Top Risks" is a structured scenario and risk ranking, and GZERO publishes retrospectives grading hits and misses. — [GZERO](https://gzeromedia.com/tag/eurasia-group-top-risks)
- **Decision trees in practice:** merger arbitrageurs break deal risk into regulatory stages (second request, suit, litigation outcome, remedy) and compare with spreads and base rates. — [Old Rope Research](https://oldroperesearch.substack.com/p/evaluating-subjective-probabilities); [Sculptor (2023)](https://www.sculptor.com/news/2023-07-15-qa-merger-arbitrage)

### Inferences
- A generic "policy decision tree" used on the Street (synthesized, not from a single source): Will it get a vote (leadership, calendar)? Will it pass each chamber (whip count, filibuster or reconciliation)? Will it be signed or vetoed? Will it survive litigation? What will implementation look like (agency discretion, timing)? Each branch gets a probability, and the product is compared with market pricing.
- Actor models are most useful when a small number of identifiable players bargain (coalition formation, a regulator's commission vote, trade negotiations). They are least useful when outcomes depend on one unpredictable decision-maker, as with 2025 tariff announcements, where "tells" and market-pain thresholds became the practical inputs.
- Red-teaming and pre-mortems are standard in intelligence tradecraft. I found no rigorous study measuring their effect on investment forecasts.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the declassified Feder article text, an independent evaluation of Bueno de Mesquita's model, or the CIA's own assessment methodology.
- No academic evaluation found of red-teaming or scenario planning accuracy in finance.
- Not researched: the selectorate theory's forecasting applications, and commercial actor-modeling firms (for example, Bueno de Mesquita's Mesquita & Roundell).

## 7. How accurate are these approaches: documented hits and misses (Brexit 2016, Trump 2016, tariffs 2025, CMS, antitrust, others)?

### Takeaway
High-profile political binaries have produced big, correlated misses by polls, models and betting markets: Brexit was priced at roughly 88–94% Remain on polling day, and Trump 2016 at 71–99% Clinton across models. Investors with *private* information (Brexit exit polls) or specific regulatory leads (Medicare Advantage 2013) profited, sometimes in legally gray ways. In 2025 the tariff cycle rewarded investors who modeled the decision-maker's reaction to market pain ("TACO") more than those who modeled stated policy. Systematic models do better on frequent, structured decisions (Supreme Court votes, FDA–adcom concordance, CMS drug selection once correct data are used) than on one-off political shocks.

### Cited Findings
- **Brexit (June 23, 2016):** Betfair said its exchange gave Remain up to a **94%** chance for much of polling day. Paddy Power was at 1/12 (about 92%), and Dow Jones put Remain odds at about 88% at poll close. Leave became the Betfair favourite only at about 3:00am. GBP fell from above $1.50 to about $1.32. — [SBC News (Betfair statement)](https://sbcnews.co.uk/europe/2016/06/24/brexit-breaks-betting-records-bettors-left-shocked/); [Bloomberg (Jun. 23, 2016)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-06-23/bookies-place-about-90-chance-on-brexit-rejection-odds-show); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2016/06/23/some-betting-markets-reverse-odds-now-predicting-uk-will-leave.html); [News Letter](https://www.newsletter.co.uk/business/pollsters-under-fire-over-hedge-fund-brexit-bets-572972)
- Some funds that bought private exit polls gained early confidence that Leave had won or that the race was closer than public pricing showed. — [News Letter](https://www.newsletter.co.uk/business/pollsters-under-fire-over-hedge-fund-brexit-bets-572972); [Bloomberg via BQ](https://www.bloombergquint.com/politics/u-k-lawmaker-says-secret-hedge-fund-brexit-polling-a-scandal)
- **US 2016:** final Clinton win probabilities were FiveThirtyEight 71.4%, Upshot about 85%, HuffPost 98.2% and PEC about 99%. — [UC Press](https://www.ucpress.edu/blog-posts/52591-lost-in-a-gallup-revisiting-the-final-hours-of-the-2016-campaign); [Univ. of Miami](https://students.com.miami.edu/reporting/forecast-models-predict-clinton-victory/)
- **US 2024:** Polymarket's heavy pro-Trump pricing, driven partly by "Théo," who reportedly made $78.7m–$85m, was vindicated by the result. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban)
- **Tariffs 2025:** Liberation Day (Apr. 2) led to a fall of almost 14% in the S&P 500 in under a week. The April 9 90-day pause for most countries (China raised to 125%) produced a rally of about 9.5%. Traders coined the "TACO trade" ("Trump Always Chickens Out"). Accounts cite bond-market turmoil (10-year yield from about 4% to 4.5%) and business pressure as triggers, while officials said the pause was strategy. — [Alaska Beacon / States Newsroom](https://alaskabeacon.com/2025/04/09/repub/trump-sets-90-day-pause-on-many-tariffs-hikes-china-to-125/); [The Global Treasurer](https://www.theglobaltreasurer.com/2025/04/10/trump-tariff-pause-market-impact/); [GlobalSecurity on "TACO"](https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/usa/government/potus-trump-taco.htm)
- **CMS drug selection (2023):** mixed accuracy. Four selected drugs were on none of five analyst lists, because analysts used stale public data. Later rounds were reportedly much more accurate (13/15). — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2023/09/cms-selects-ten-drugs-for-inflation-reduction-act); [BioXconomy](https://www.bioxconomy.com/pricing-and-contracting/medicare-names-2028-drug-list-with-two-surprises)
- **Supreme Court:** models score about 70–75%, versus about 59% for experts in the 2002 Term study. — [Ruger et al. 2004](https://openscholarship.wustl.edu/law_scholarship/464); [Katz et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03473v2)
- **FDA:** the advisory committee vote is a strong but imperfect predictor (88% concordance). Approvals after negative votes are the main source of surprises. — [JAMA Health Forum 2023](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama-health-forum/fullarticle/2807050)
- **Antitrust (2021–2024):** merger-arb spreads widened under aggressive FTC/DOJ enforcement. Reuters Breakingviews later described the Khan/Kanter push as having hurt arb returns, and DOJ court losses (Change Healthcare, US Sugar) partly reversed spreads. — [Bloomberg/BQ survey](https://www.bloombergquint.com/business/watchdog-crackdown-casts-shadow-over-deals-boom-m-a-survey); [Breakingviews via Tiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2483002137); [Amundi via IBKR](https://ibkrcampus.com/?p=183189)
- **Experts in general:** Tetlock's experts did little better than simple algorithms (2005). Superforecasters beat IC analysts by about 30% (approximate, secondary). — [Jason Collins](https://corporate.jasoncollins.blog/expert-political-judgment); [Wikipedia: Superforecaster](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/Superforecaster)

### Inferences
- The "misses" share features. They were binary, one-off events with correlated polling error and herding: models, markets and bookmakers used the same polls. The "hits" came from either (a) better or private data (exit polls, early leaks) or (b) better models of a specific decision-maker's incentives (bond-market pain thresholds in 2025).
- For a learner: forecast accuracy should be judged with proper scoring rules (Brier or log score) over many forecasts, not single headline calls. A 71% forecast that "misses" is not necessarily wrong. FiveThirtyEight's 2016 number was the most cautious of the major models, and it was penalized least by the outcome under a Brier score (1 − 0.714 → Brier of about 0.51, versus about 0.98 for a 99% forecast).
- The policy-forecasting edge in 2025–2026 appears to have shifted from *Congress-centric* PI (vote counts, committee staff) toward *executive-centric* inference (presidential statements, deadlines, market-pain thresholds, court challenges to executive actions) and to leak-sensitive prediction markets.

- Not covered: Liberation Day forecasts *ex ante* (what sell-side policy teams predicted before April 2, 2025), and the IEEPA tariff litigation outcome and how markets and forecasters priced it.
- No retrieved, verified data on hedge-fund P&L from Brexit or on how many funds used private exit polls.
- Non-US policy forecasting (China, EU) was not researched in depth beyond Brexit.
